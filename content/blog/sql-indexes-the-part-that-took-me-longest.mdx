Indexes were the first database thing I could not learn from reading. Every
tutorial showed `CREATE INDEX` and said it makes queries faster, and I believed
it, and then I added three of them to a table and a write-heavy job got slower.

That is the whole post, really, but let me be specific about what I got wrong.

## The mental model I had

"Reads are common, writes are rare, so index everything you read by." That is a
reasonable-sounding model and it is wrong often enough to cost you an afternoon.

An index is not free. Every insert has to update it. Every update has to move it.
On a table with heavy write traffic, an index on a low-cardinality column can make
inserts measurably slower while barely helping any query, because the planner
decides a sequential scan is cheaper than walking the index anyway.

## What actually helped

**Look at the query plan before you index anything.** `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` on the
real query. I had been indexing based on which columns appeared in `WHERE`, which
tells you nothing about cardinality. `WHERE status = 'active'` on a million rows
is not selective. `WHERE id = $1` always is.

**Index the columns you sort and join on, not just the ones you filter on.** My
biggest win came from a composite index matching the exact order of a `WHERE` plus
an `ORDER BY`, which let Postgres stop sorting entirely.

**Watch what you unindexed.** When a slow query was fixed, I dropped the index I
had added for it months earlier and nothing changed. It had been pure cost.

**Trust the measurements over the intuition.** My intuition said the job was slow
because of the database. The plan said it was slow because of a sequential scan
on a table that had not been vacuumed since a bulk load.

## The part I actually needed to hear

An index is not an optimisation you add. It is a structure you pay for on every
write, in exchange for not paying a cost on some reads. Whether that trade is
good depends entirely on the ratio of your reads to your writes, which is a fact
about your application that no amount of reasoning about the query will tell you.

Once I stopped thinking of it as free speed and started thinking of it as a
bet, it stopped being confusing. You do not place a bet you cannot justify, and
`EXPLAIN ANALYZE` is how you check whether you won.
